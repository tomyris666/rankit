The Slopocalypse

AI slop isn't the problem — it never was. The real question is whether what you make actually means something.

April 21, 2026 5 min read
Back to News

AI slop is not new. It is an ongoing degradation of the internet — one that predates large language models by decades. Fake content is not inherently bad content. Meaningless content is.

The conversation around AI content has always been conducted on the wrong terrain.

The first content spinner, Articlebot, launched in 2005. Its creator claimed it was built to protect free speech from search engine censorship. It was used overwhelmingly as a black hat SEO tool — churning out thousands of near-identical pages in hopes that a few would rank. This practice of algorithmically generated content has been evolving for twenty years.

Over the past three years, many clung to the notion that EEAT — Google's framework for evaluating expertise, experience, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness — meant that organic content would take precedence. They were right that quality would win. They were wrong about what quality means. By the three metrics of cost, speed, and scale, AI is the definitive winner. Quality is where the debate is ongoing, and it is worth noting: the majority of AI-generated content is an order of magnitude superior to the spam that plagued the internet before it, and the SERP results show it.

Which is why the question "Can AI-generated content be superior to human-made content?" is itself the problem. It assumes origin is the relevant variable.

What Makes Content Slop?

For written content, can anything be more infuriating than reading the format of "not just a _, it's a _"? Why is it that the mere smell of LLM content induces repulsion in the average reader?

Not because it was made by a machine. Rather, it is a signpost that screams: you are about to read something that zero thought was put into.

At a glance, two pieces of content can have exactly the same output. Consider a video of a ballerina performing an eight-turn pirouette, indistinguishable from an AI-generated one. For most, simply the knowledge that it is artificial degrades the enjoyment. This is because the pirouette is not the point in itself. The meaning of such a turn is the human condition in pursuit of perfection — the blood, sweat and tears that culminated in the gravity-defying act of the dance. It is the meaning behind the spin from which pleasure is derived.

The revulsion is not proof that AI content fails. In this context, it has simply failed to grasp the source from which utility is derived for the consumer.

A cruder example: the thirst traps that inundate most social platforms. It is not enough to check boxes of beautiful curves and a pleasant face — they are selling the dream of seduction. No matter how small the probability of attaining the company of your desires, this is precisely what drives your interest. Strip the meaning, and the content dies.

Why else do content spammers spend thousands of tokens ensuring each frame contains five fingers on each hand? Nobody is looking there. But the illusion must be upheld.

Origin is irrelevant. Meaning is paramount.

Stop The Slop

To employ AI to its fullest potential, you will — to the undoubted dismay of clout chasers and social gurus — have to put genuine effort and personal value into what you make. Just as interest in painted portraits was not killed by the modern camera but forced painters to find what photography could not capture, people will have to learn how to elevate old forms of the art to new heights. Those who choose to employ these technologies will need to generate novel ways of expression.

It's 2026, and good time we put to rest the debate of AI vs. human. We have already passed the point where the majority of people can tell the difference. Focus on whether your output actually means something.

Otherwise, all you are making is slop.

And slop stinks.
